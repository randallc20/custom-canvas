### P2 — The `checkout.session.completed` handler discards every read error, so a transient failure is acted on as "row absent": the listing read detaches the order from its listing and leaves the piece on sale, and the two order lookups can route a redelivered payment into the oversell branch and refund a real sale
**Where:** `src/app/api/webhooks/stripe/route.ts:301-305` (listing `.single()`, error dropped), `:346-370, 387` (null listing → "listing vanished" → `detachParty`), `:335-339` and `:417-421` (both order lookups, error dropped), `:428-446` (oversell refund), `:317-319` (the resume's listing re-read); contrast `:625-637`, where R13 gave the dispute handlers exactly this check.
**What happens:** A buyer pays. The handler's first statement reads the listing with `.single()` and keeps only `data`. Supabase's client does not throw on a network or pooler error; it resolves `{ data: null, error }` (`postgrest-js/dist/index.cjs:155`), which is what the R13 comment at `:625-628` says and why the dispute handlers now read `error`. Here `listing` is null, so the R14 "listing vanished mid-Checkout" branch runs: Sentry gets "missing listing", the artist is looked up from metadata, `detachParty(order, 'listing_id')` writes the order with `listing_id: null`, and `completeSale` skips the sold update because there is no listing id. The order exists and the buyer is confirmed for "an artwork (listing since removed)", but the listing is still `available` in the feed, and the order row is outside `orders_one_live_per_listing` (a null key never conflicts). A second collector buys the same piece: checkout passes (`status === 'available'`), the insert succeeds, the artist is transferred twice for one artwork, and neither buyer is auto-refunded. The reconcile cron sees a row per payment and reports nothing. Detection exists (the Sentry error and the "deleted listing" admin notification from `completeSale`), but the state is already written, and the repair — refund one buyer, restore `listing_id` by SQL — is manual. The same conflation in the two `orders` lookups has a worse branch: Stripe redelivers an event whose first attempt 500'd during a database blip; the `existingOrder` read fails (null), the insert hits 23505 on the payment-intent unique index, the `samePayment` read fails too (null), and the handler concludes "oversell": a full refund with `reverse_transfer` is issued on a valid, already-recorded order, the audit insert fails on the same unique index (logged, acked), and the buyer is emailed "sold moments before your payment" while their Orders page says Paid and the artist ships. That needs two failed reads and one successful insert in one invocation, which is rarer, but Stripe retries exactly when the previous attempt failed, so the blip and the redelivery are correlated. The resume path's re-read (`:317-319`) is the mild case: a failed read acks the event with the listing still `available` and no emails sent, and nothing later notices a `paid` order on an `available` listing.
**Why it's real:** I read the client library's failure path: with `shouldThrowOnError` false, a fetch rejection is caught and returned as `{ data: null, error: { message, details, code: '' } }`. The listing branch decides on `!listing` alone (`:356`), the redelivery pre-check on `existingOrder` alone (`:340`), and the oversell decision on `samePayment` alone (`:422`). PGRST116 ("no rows") and a connection reset look identical to all three. The R13 commit fixed the dispute handlers' reads for this reason and did not touch the checkout handler; R14 then added the null-listing branch on top of the unchecked read, which turned a retry-forever (the old behaviour on a missing listing) into a silent detach.
**Fix direction:** Read `error` on all five lookups; treat `code === 'PGRST116'` (or a null with no error on `maybeSingle`) as "no row" and anything else as `retryLater`. The listing branch should only detach when the row is confirmed absent.

### P2 — Seller-protection requirement 4 has no writer anywhere: `signature_confirmed` is frozen for everyone but the service role and nothing service-side ever sets it, so every order of $750 or more is ineligible by construction while the ship modal and the badge tell the artist how to satisfy it
**Where:** `supabase/migrations/00040_seller_protection.sql:48-52` (frozen, "service-role only until a carrier integration can confirm it"); `src/utils/evaluateProtection.ts:131-134`; `src/app/api/payments/checkout/route.ts:93, 177` (`signature_required` snapshotted at $750); `src/components/studio/SalesSection.tsx:220-222` (ship modal: "select it when you buy the label"); `src/components/studio/ProtectionBadge.tsx:20-27, 45` (only the photo and notes failures are "frozen"; the signature failure is listed under "To protect this order:"); `src/types/order.ts:37` and `00040:34` (`waived` exists in the CHECK and is used nowhere).
**What happens:** An artist sells a $1,200 piece. The Ship Order modal says the order is $750 or more "so signature confirmation is also required — select it when you buy the label". They buy USPS Signature Confirmation, ship on day two with tracking, mark it delivered, reply to every message. The Studio badge still reads "Not protected yet" with "Signature confirmation is required on orders of $750 or more" under "To protect this order:", and there is nothing on the order, in Studio, in `/admin`, in `scripts/`, in a migration or in the runbook that sets `signature_confirmed`. A non-receipt chargeback lands. `assessProtection` reads `signature_confirmed = false`, freezes `ineligible`, and the artist is told "This order was not Protected, so the amount will be deducted from your payout. Reasons: Signature confirmation is required on orders of $750 or more." The dispute is lost and $1,020 is reversed from an artist who did every single thing the platform asked. The same is true of every order at or above $750 the platform will ever take. Below $750 the requirement does not apply, so the first pass's staging verification (a lower-value purchase) would not have shown it.
**Why it's real:** `grep -rn signature_confirmed src scripts supabase/migrations docs` finds one reader (`ProtectionBadge`), the webhook's assessment, the freeze in every guard version, and the spec's data-model row ("Proof of signature on delivery"). No route, cron, admin page, SQL script or runbook step writes it. `00040`'s own comment says the column is service-role only *until a carrier integration can confirm it*, and no such integration or interim admin path exists. This is the same shape as requirement 3 before ruling D1 — an evidence input the artist cannot produce — except D1 recorded a decision and changed the artist-facing wording, and requirement 4 has neither: `DECISIONS.md` is silent, the modal actively instructs the artist, and the badge lists it as fixable.
**Fix direction:** Decide it, the way D1 was decided. Either an admin-only route that sets `signature_confirmed` (service role, after the admin checks the carrier's signature record; a runbook step at dispute time), or waive requirement 4 at launch (`protection_status = 'waived'` already exists, or simply skip the check when no confirmation path exists) and record the ruling. Whichever way, the modal and the badge must stop telling artists to do something the platform cannot record.

### P2 — `account.updated` drops any event whose `created` second equals the last one recorded, so a same-second burst at onboarding completion can leave `stripe_onboarded` false with nothing left to flip it (UNVERIFIED on the burst)
**Where:** `src/app/api/webhooks/stripe/route.ts:1113-1142` (`stripe_account_updated_at.lt.<event.created>`, second resolution); `supabase/migrations/00056:191` (the column R14 added); `src/app/api/payments/checkout/route.ts:70-72` (the only consumer).
**What happens:** An artist finishes Express onboarding. Stripe emits several `account.updated` events as verification completes: one where `transfers` is still `pending` and `payouts_enabled` is false, then one where both are active. Stripe's `event.created` is an integer second. If the two carry the same second and the "not ready" event is processed first, it writes `stripe_onboarded = false` and stamps that second; the "ready" event's `lt` compare fails, zero rows come back, and the handler logs "skipped: stale" and acks. `stripe_onboarded` is written nowhere else (grepped). Checkout tells every buyer "This artist has not finished setting up payments yet" until Stripe next changes the account, which for a finished account may be never. Before R14 the last event won, so this was self-correcting; R14 fixed the auth pass's stale-`false` appendix item by adding the gate, and the gate introduced this.
**Why it's real:** The compare is strict (`lt`) on a value with one-second resolution, and `Stripe.Event.created` is documented as a Unix timestamp in seconds. What I cannot confirm from code is whether Stripe actually emits two `account.updated` events for one connected account within the same second during onboarding. What settles it: open the staging Connect endpoint's event log for one completed onboarding and compare `created` on consecutive `account.updated` events; or watch Sentry for the "skipped: ... a newer event is already recorded" info line after a test onboarding. If they never share a second, this drops to the appendix.
**Fix direction:** Do what Stripe recommends for out-of-order events: on `account.updated`, `accounts.retrieve(account.id)` and write `ready` from the live object, keeping the timestamp only as an optimisation (`lte`, so an equal second is not discarded). The live read makes the ordering question moot.

### P3 — A transient count failure at checkout snapshots `evidence_photo_count = 0` into the session, freezing an ineligible verdict on requirement 5 for the life of the order
**Where:** `src/app/api/payments/checkout/route.ts:87-91` (`photoCount ?? 0`, error dropped), `:174` (written to metadata), `src/utils/orderRecord.ts:168` (recorded on the order), `supabase/migrations/00057:57` (frozen).
**What happens:** A buyer checks out on a listing with five photographs. The `listing_images` count query hits a pooler error; `count` is null and becomes 0. The session metadata says `evidence_photo_count: 0`; the webhook records it; the guard freezes it. The Studio badge shows "The listing had fewer than 3 photographs at the time of sale" under "Fixed at the time of sale — can't be changed for this order". Months later a chargeback is lost and the payout is reversed on a requirement the listing met. This is the checkout-time twin of the second pass's P2 (a transient evidence read frozen as `ineligible`), which R13 fixed at dispute time by retrying; the snapshot site was not given the same treatment.
**Why it's real:** The count is the only evidence input read from a separate query (the condition-notes check uses the listing row already loaded), it is read with the result's `error` discarded, and nothing later re-derives it (the snapshot exists precisely so the live listing is never consulted). A null count and zero photographs are indistinguishable downstream.
**Fix direction:** Read `{ count, error }`; on error return the existing 502 "could not start checkout, try again" instead of creating a session with worst-case evidence. One line.

### P3 — `charge.refunded` on a `disputed` order derives "was it shipped" from the frozen status, so an out-of-order full refund on a frozen delivered order marks it refunded and relists a piece the buyer already has
**Where:** `src/app/api/webhooks/stripe/route.ts:540` (`wasShipped` from `status`), `:572` (unconditional `refunded` write), `:585-603` (relist when not shipped); contrast `src/app/api/admin/orders/[id]/refund/route.ts:45-50` (the settle route refuses `disputed`, so it never reaches its own `:113`).
**What happens:** A piece is delivered. The admin issues a full dashboard refund with the reversal box ticked while the webhook endpoint is failing (rotated secret). The buyer's bank files a chargeback on the refunded charge anyway (Stripe permits it; the first pass's second P1 was built on this). Both events retry independently. `charge.dispute.created` lands first: the row is `delivered` with no `stripe_refund_id`, so it is a `chargeback` — frozen as `disputed`, `pre_dispute_status: 'delivered'`. `charge.refunded` then lands: `order.status` is `disputed`, so `wasShipped` is false; the row becomes `refunded` (with `pre_dispute_status`, `dispute_status` and the frozen verdict left in place) and, because no other live order holds the listing and it is still `sold`, the listing goes back to `available`. A piece that is physically with the buyer is in the feed. `charge.dispute.closed` arrives (`won` on the refund evidence, or `charge_refunded`): the row is not `disputed`, so it is "nothing to restore" — and the `charge_refunded` variant tells the artist "Bank inquiry closed". Nobody is told the piece was relisted.
**Why it's real:** `wasShipped` is computed from the current status only; `shipped_at`, `delivered_at` and `pre_dispute_status` are all on the row and all say "shipped". The settle route has the same derivation but is unreachable on `disputed`; the webhook has no such gate. Stripe refuses refunds during an open chargeback, so this cannot happen in order — it is strictly the outage-plus-reordering path, which is why it is P3.
**Fix direction:** Derive it from evidence, not status: `wasShipped = !!shipped_at || !!delivered_at` (pickup orders that reached `delivered` have `delivered_at`), and null `pre_dispute_status` when this path closes a frozen order. Two lines.

## Not findings

- **Prior-pass fixes, re-read against callers.** Inquiry lifecycle, post-refund chargeback, post-refund escalation (`dispute_status`), `closed`-before-`created` (`needs_assessment` assesses, CAS on `pending`, re-reads), closed-status open events, resent open events recognised by outcome, refund evidence outranking `pre_dispute_status`, CAS'd restore, `disputed` in the one-live-order index, listing PATCH refusing `sold → available` with a live order, oversold-buyer email and notification, `shipping_address`/`tracking_number`/`carrier` freezes, `delivered_at` stamped once, second dispute reversing only the remainder from `transfer.amount_reversed`, null-party orders (R14) inserted after FK detachment with loud admin and Sentry notices. All present and correct.
- **Payout math.** `calcSplit` is exactly the locked rule: commission = round(15% of price), payout = price − commission + shipping, fee = ceil((2.9% × (price + shipping) + 30¢) / 0.971). $100 → $3.30, $1,000 → $30.18, $5,000 → $149.64 reproduce; `transfer_data.amount` is the same locked payout the webhook records from metadata; conservation holds for every case tried; `Math.round` never disagrees with integer half-up for any price to $100,000.
- **Refund math.** `calculateRefundSplit` returns price + shipping + their proportional tax; fee and fee-tax retained; the reversal is exactly `artist_payout_cents`; the settle refund is always partial (fee ≥ 31¢) so `charge.refunded` never double-handles it; each Stripe mutation is idempotency-keyed and its id persisted before the next step; the close is CAS'd and asserted.
- **Duplicate and concurrent `checkout.session.completed`.** Payment-intent pre-check, `orders_payment_intent_unique`, the 23505 same-payment branch, and `resumeIfIncomplete` gated on the order still being `paid` converge when the reads succeed (the read-failure case is the P2 above).
- **`charge.refunded` / `charge.dispute.*` before the order exists.** 409 and Stripe retries; oversell audit rows count as a match.
- **Webhook never delivered.** The reconcile cron lists 7-day payments (no-order check), refunds and disputes by event time, and every `disputed` row regardless of age; alerts batch to admins and Sentry; the runbook says how to replay. A `paid` order whose `completeSale` never ran is the one shape it does not see (appendix of the P2 above).
- **Order state machine, non-privileged.** Only `paid|shipped → shipped`; `delivered` only through `mark-delivered` (CAS on `shipped`) or `confirm-pickup` (both stamps); every money, party, evidence, dispute and stamp column frozen; buyers have no UPDATE policy; INSERT policy dropped. No client sequence reaches or leaves `refunded`/`disputed`. `approve-refund`: artist-only, CAS on null, refuses non-open statuses; `confirm-pickup`: per-party atomic stamp, self-healing promotion, refuses `disputed` and refund-in-flight; `notify-shipped`: once-only claim.
- **Commission state machine.** `pending → quoted → in_progress → delivered → confirmed`; `pending|quoted → cancelled`; `in_progress|delivered → disputed → (withdraw) pre-status | (admin) confirmed|cancelled`. Every route ownership-checks under the caller's RLS and CASes on the status it read; `commissionQuoteSchema` carries only the three quote fields; no client UPDATE policy; INSERT guarded (R14); `updates` refuses closed states; no money moves. `accepted`/`completed` exist in the type with no writer — harmless.
- **Checkout while the listing changes.** Every amount, the pickup flag and the evidence snapshot are locked in session metadata; `buildOrderRecord` never reads the live row; a hidden listing still becomes `sold`; a price edit mid-session charges the session's price. Listing deleted mid-Checkout: recorded detached with a loud notice (R14). Sold to someone else mid-Checkout: index plus idempotency-keyed full refund with `reverse_transfer`, now with buyer and admin notices; `charge_already_refunded` on a late resend is treated as success.
- **Refund after payout.** The reversal pulls from the connected balance; the 14-day payout delay is the settled cover. `approve-refund` has no time bound, so a refund approved weeks after delivery relies on Stripe debiting a negative balance — a consequence of the recorded decision, not a defect in it.
- **Lost dispute on a protected order.** Absorbed, nothing reversed; on an ineligible order the reversal is `min(dispute.amount, payout − amount_reversed)`, keyed on the dispute id, skipped when the transfer is already fully reversed.
- **Account deletion.** Refused while party to `paid`/`shipped`/`disputed` or an unsettled approved refund; money rows outlive their parties; both dispute handlers and `completeSale` tolerate null parties loudly.
- **`stripe-connect`.** Idempotency key per artist row; row write asserted; account deliberately kept on a failed write; `account.updated` requires `payouts_enabled` and active `transfers` (the ordering gate is the P2 above).
- **Signature verification.** Both endpoint secrets tried, raw body, SDK tolerance; `event.account` unchecked remains the r3 appendix item.
- **Stripe API version** `2026-02-25.clover` with stripe@20: `collected_information.shipping_details` and `total_details.amount_tax` ride the session payload.
- **Settled decisions left alone.** Destination charges, Express accounts, fee pass-through and its uncovered components, the payments gate, pickup tax sourcing (D2), artist delivery attestation (D1), commission dispute exits (D5), the 14-day payout delay.
