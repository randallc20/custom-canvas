### P1 — An inquiry-type dispute leaves the order in `disputed` forever, and tells the artist their payout is being deducted
**Where:** `src/app/api/webhooks/stripe/route.ts:384-457` (created), `:459-536` (closed, branch selection at `:471-521`)
**What happens:** A card network inquiry (Amex and Discover retrievals, and Visa's pre-dispute inquiries) arrives at Stripe as a Dispute object with status `warning_needs_response`, and Stripe fires `charge.dispute.created` for it. The handler marks the order `disputed`, freezes a protection verdict, and sends the artist "the amount will be deducted from your payout" even though an inquiry withdraws no funds. When the inquiry closes without escalating, Stripe fires `charge.dispute.closed` with status `warning_closed`. The handler has an `if (lost)` branch and an `else if (status === 'won')` branch and nothing else, so the order stays `disputed` with a `dispute_id`. From there: the buyer cannot review (`reviews` policy needs `delivered`), the artist cannot approve a refund (`approve-refund/route.ts:25` needs paid/shipped/delivered), neither party can confirm a pickup handoff (`confirm-pickup/route.ts:48`), the Studio shows a red "Disputed" badge with no button, and the admin orders page has no status control at all. Nothing in the app can move it.
**Why it's real:** Stripe's event reference states `charge.dispute.closed` fires on `lost`, `warning_closed`, or `won`; only two of the three are handled. I grepped every writer of `orders.status`: the webhook, the admin refund route (refuses nothing but only writes `refunded`), `confirm-pickup`, and the artist's client-side update, which the `00042` guard limits to `shipped`/`delivered`. None is reachable from `disputed` through the UI.
**Fix direction:** In `charge.dispute.created`, treat `warning_*` statuses as an inquiry: notify without changing status, or record it in a separate column. In `charge.dispute.closed`, make "not lost" the restore branch rather than "won" only.

### P1 — A chargeback filed after a settled refund flips the refunded order back to `paid`
**Where:** `src/app/api/webhooks/stripe/route.ts:394-415` (no `refunded` guard), `:511-521` (blind restore); `src/app/api/admin/orders/[id]/refund/route.ts:101-117`; `src/app/admin/orders/page.tsx:100`
**What happens:** The admin settles an artist-approved refund: the buyer gets price + shipping + tax back, the payout is reversed, the order becomes `refunded`, and an unshipped listing is relisted. The buyer's bank files a chargeback anyway (a refund in transit, or a buyer who double-dips; Stripe permits disputes on refunded charges). `charge.dispute.created` checks only `status === 'disputed'`, so the refunded order becomes `disputed`, protection is re-evaluated, and the artist is told the amount will come out of a payout that was already reversed. Stripe then closes the dispute as `won` on the refund evidence. The `won` branch restores `status: order.delivered_at ? 'delivered' : 'paid'`. The refunded order is now `paid`: the admin page's financial totals count it as revenue (`settled` excludes only `refunded`), the artist's Sales tab offers "Mark as Shipped" on a sale that was unwound, and the buyer's Orders page shows "Paid" for money they got back. If the relisted piece was resold in the meantime, the update to `paid` collides with `orders_one_live_per_listing`; the error is not checked, so the order instead sticks at `disputed`.
**Why it's real:** Neither handler reads `stripe_refund_id`, `stripe_reversal_id` or `refund_approved_at`, all of which are set by the settle route and would identify the case. The money itself is safe: the `lost` branch skips the reversal because `stripe_reversal_id` is set. It is the record that is corrupted. Recovery exists only by accident: the "Settle refund" button reappears because `refund_approved_at` is set and status is no longer `refunded`, and clicking it skips both Stripe steps and rewrites `refunded`. A dashboard-initiated refund has no such button.
**Fix direction:** In `charge.dispute.created`, if `stripe_refund_id` is set (or status is `refunded`), record the dispute id and notify admins but leave status alone. In the `won` branch, restore to `refunded` when a refund id exists, otherwise to the pre-dispute status, which is worth persisting in a column at dispute time rather than inferring from `delivered_at`.

### P2 — Seller-protection requirement 3, "the carrier confirmed delivery", is satisfied by the artist clicking a button, and `delivered_at` is not a frozen column
**Where:** `src/utils/evaluateProtection.ts:124-127`; `src/components/studio/SalesSection.tsx:94-101, 173-175`; `supabase/migrations/00022_review_fixes_phase79.sql:5-17`; `supabase/migrations/00042_ai_disclosure_and_pickup_handoff.sql:40-84`; `docs/SELLER_PROTECTION_SPEC.md:23` vs `:46`
**What happens:** An artist ships with USPS tracking inside the window on a listing that had three photos and long notes, replies to messages, and clicks "Mark Delivered" the same afternoon. The `orders_set_delivered_at` trigger stamps `delivered_at`. The package is lost; the buyer files a non-receipt chargeback. `assessProtection` sees every requirement met and persists `protected`. The dispute is lost, the `lost` branch skips the reversal, and Custom Canvas absorbs price + shipping while the artist keeps the payout. The Studio badge actively coaches this: an artist who has not clicked sees "Delivery was never confirmed" listed as a fixable failure. Separately, the `00042` guard freezes `shipped_at`, `signature_confirmed` and every snapshot column but not `delivered_at`, so an artist can also set it directly through the REST endpoint on a `shipped` order without changing status.
**Why it's real:** `00040`'s own comment explains why `signature_confirmed` is service-role only: "letting an artist self-attest ... would let them shift their own losses onto Custom Canvas." Requirement 3 is the same shape of evidence, worth more (it applies to every order, not just those over $750), and is self-attested. The spec's data-model table (`:46`) does list "artist confirmation" as a source for `delivered_at`, so this may be a launch-scope choice, but it is not in `DECISIONS.md`, and the artist-facing rule text (`:23`) says the carrier confirms.
**Fix direction:** Either make requirement 3 need something the artist cannot produce alone (buyer confirmation, the way pickup already works, or a carrier lookup), or record in `DECISIONS.md` that artist attestation is accepted at launch and change the artist-facing wording to match. In either case freeze `delivered_at` in the guard and stamp it server-side like `shipped_at`.

### P2 — Refund and dispute events for a payment with no order are acked and dropped, and nothing ever reconciles orders against Stripe
**Where:** `src/app/api/webhooks/stripe/route.ts:323-328, 394-399, 464-469`; `vercel.json` (four crons, none touch Stripe)
**What happens:** The webhook endpoint is broken for a few hours (a rotated signing secret, a bad deploy; the route already had one such outage per the comment at `:99-105`). During the outage a buyer pays. Stripe queues `checkout.session.completed` for retry. The buyer emails support; the admin issues a full refund with transfer reversal from the Stripe dashboard. `charge.refunded` is queued too. Retries are independent per event, so `charge.refunded` can land first: no order matches, `break`, HTTP 200, event done. `checkout.session.completed` then lands, inserts a `paid` order, marks the listing `sold`, and emails both parties a confirmation for a sale that was refunded an hour ago. The order and listing stay wrong indefinitely. The same ack-and-drop applies to `charge.dispute.created` and `.closed`. In the other direction, if `checkout.session.completed` exhausts Stripe's retry window (the deliberate 500 on a deleted listing at `:158-168` loops until it does), the buyer's money and the artist's transfer exist only in Stripe. No cron, script or admin view lists Stripe payments and diffs them against `orders`.
**Why it's real:** Every `if (!order) break;` returns 200. I grepped `src/` and `scripts/` for `reconcil`, `sessions.list`, `paymentIntents.list`, and `charges.list`; the only Stripe list-style call anywhere is none. The prior review noted the buyer-facing side of the delay; this is the state side.
**Fix direction:** Return a non-2xx when a money event references a payment intent with no order, so Stripe retries it after the order exists (the oversell path is safe with this: its audit row carries the same payment intent and is written moments after the refund). Add a small daily cron that lists succeeded payment intents from the last few days and alerts on any without an order, and any `refunded`/`disputed` Stripe state that disagrees with the row.

### P2 — The order status guard checks the target state, not the transition, so an artist can move a `refunded` or `disputed` order to `shipped`/`delivered` and re-occupy the listing's one-live-order slot
**Where:** `supabase/migrations/00042_ai_disclosure_and_pickup_handoff.sql:78-80`; `supabase/migrations/00002_missing_tables_and_policies.sql:195` (UPDATE policy, no `WITH CHECK`); `src/app/api/webhooks/stripe/route.ts:195-231`
**What happens:** An order is refunded and its listing relisted. The artist, using the anon key and their own session against the PostgREST endpoint (the same call `services/orders.ts` makes), sets `status = 'delivered'`. The guard's only status rule is `NEW.status NOT IN ('shipped','delivered')`, so the write succeeds; `orders_one_live_per_listing` now holds this listing. The next buyer pays; the webhook insert fails 23505, the same-payment lookup finds nothing, the branch concludes "oversell", refunds the new buyer with transfer reversal, and writes an audit row. The listing stays `available` and every future buyer of it is auto-refunded, each time costing the platform Stripe's non-returnable processing fee. The same write on a `disputed` order re-enables `approve-refund` and `confirm-pickup` (both accept `delivered`), which the dispute path deliberately locks.
**Why it's real:** The UI only offers the transitions it should, but the guard is the actual boundary and it admits `refunded → delivered` and `disputed → shipped`. The 2026-08-25 fix restored the guard as a target-state check; the transition check was never there.
**Fix direction:** One line in the guard: when not privileged, a status change is allowed only if `OLD.status IN ('paid','shipped')`. Ship it with the db-smoke expectations per `CONVENTIONS.md`.

### P2 — Pickup orders are taxed on the card's billing address, not the Houston handoff (mechanism verified, tax treatment UNVERIFIED)
**Where:** `src/app/api/payments/checkout/route.ts:74-82, 138-144`
**What happens:** A collector with an out-of-state card buys a pickup-only piece. `shipping_address_collection` is omitted for pickup, so Stripe Tax falls through its precedence list to the billing address. Stripe's documentation states the shipping address is used if present, otherwise the billing address and further fallbacks. The session is taxed for the card's state (or not at all), while the artwork changes hands in Texas with the platform as the Texas-registered merchant of record. The comment at `:139-143` describes this exact bug for shipped orders ("a buyer with an out-of-state card shipping into Houston was charged $0 Texas tax while the platform ... still owed it") and fixes it by collecting the destination; pickup was left on the old sourcing.
**Why it's real:** The precedence rule is verified against Stripe's docs and there is no other address on the session. What I cannot verify from code is whether Texas treats an in-person handoff as sourced to the handoff location for a marketplace provider; that is a question for counsel. What would settle the mechanism: create one test-mode pickup session with a non-Texas billing postal code and read `total_details.amount_tax`.
**Fix direction:** If counsel confirms origin sourcing for pickup, give Stripe a Texas location for pickup sessions (collect a billing address restricted to US and pass the artist's pickup city/ZIP via the Tax API, or collect the address anyway labelled as the pickup location). If counsel says billing sourcing is acceptable, record that in `DECISIONS.md` alongside the shipping decision.

### P2 — A dispute won on an in-transit order regresses it from `shipped` to `paid`, and re-marking it shipped overwrites the fulfilment-window evidence
**Where:** `src/app/api/webhooks/stripe/route.ts:511-521`; `supabase/migrations/00042_ai_disclosure_and_pickup_handoff.sql:70-74`; `src/components/studio/SalesSection.tsx:170-175`
**What happens:** A buyer disputes non-receipt while the package is in transit (the most common dispute on a marketplace). The artist's tracking wins it. The `won` branch restores `delivered_at ? 'delivered' : 'paid'`; `shipped_at` is set but `delivered_at` is not, so the order becomes `paid`. The buyer's Orders page drops from "Shipped" to "Paid"; the artist's Sales tab shows "Mark as Shipped" again. The artist clicks it: the guard's `NEW.status = 'shipped' AND OLD.status IS DISTINCT FROM 'shipped'` branch stamps `shipped_at := now()`, weeks after the sale. The Studio badge now reports "Shipped after the 5-business-day fulfillment window", the buyer gets a second "your order shipped" email via `notify-shipped`, and if the order were ever assessed again the artist would be ineligible on a requirement they met.
**Why it's real:** The restore has only two outcomes and neither is `shipped`. `shipped_at` is not consulted. The re-stamp is unconditional on the transition.
**Fix direction:** Restore `shipped` when `shipped_at` is set and `delivered_at` is not (or persist the pre-dispute status, as in the second P1). Stamp `shipped_at` only when it is null.

### P2 — A disputed commission has no exit anywhere in the app
**Where:** `src/app/api/commissions/[id]/dispute/route.ts:27-34`; `src/components/commission/CommissionPanel.tsx:171-197`; `src/app/admin/disputes/page.tsx:53` (operates on `reports`)
**What happens:** A requester disputes an in-progress or delivered commission. Status becomes `disputed`. Enumerating every writer: `accept` needs `pending`, `confirm` needs `quoted`/`delivered`, `complete` needs `accepted`/`in_progress`, `decline` needs `pending`/`quoted`, `dispute` needs `in_progress`/`delivered`, the nudge cron only writes `last_nudge_at`. The `00009` migration removed the client UPDATE policy. The admin "disputes" page manages the `reports` table and never reads `commissions`. The panel renders no action for either party once status is `disputed`. The commission, its conversation banner and the artist's queue carry a red badge permanently; the artist cannot deliver, the requester cannot confirm or withdraw, and no admin can close it. No money moves in commissions, so this is a stuck workflow rather than a stuck payment.
**Why it's real:** I grepped every `from('commissions')` write in `src/` and every admin surface; the list above is complete.
**Fix direction:** Add an admin resolve route (`disputed → confirmed | cancelled` with a `closed_reason`) surfaced on an admin commissions list, and let the requester withdraw their own dispute back to the prior state.

### P3 — Stripe Connect account creation is not tied to the row write, so a failed write orphans the account and strands onboarding
**Where:** `src/app/api/payments/stripe-connect/route.ts:23-46`
**What happens:** `accounts.create` succeeds, then the `artist_profiles` update fails or returns zero rows (transient DB error, or the row was just deleted by the account-deletion cascade noted in review 01). The result is not checked. The artist is sent to onboard on the new account; `account.updated` for it matches no `stripe_account_id`, so `stripe_onboarded` never flips and checkout keeps refusing them. Their next click creates a second Express account under the same email and onboards that one, leaving the first with the artist's KYC data.
**Why it's real:** The write is server-side so the client-write convention does not literally apply, but its spirit does: a write whose failure is silent while a real-world side effect has already happened. There is no idempotency key on `accounts.create` either.
**Fix direction:** Assert the affected row and, on failure, delete the just-created account (or persist first with a placeholder and create second). Pass an idempotency key derived from the artist id.

## Not findings

- Duplicate `checkout.session.completed`: pre-check on payment intent, `orders_payment_intent_unique`, and the 23505 same-payment branch. A concurrent duplicate blocks on the unique index until the first commits, so the same-payment lookup finds the row.
- Oversell: `orders_one_live_per_listing` plus an idempotency-keyed full refund with `reverse_transfer`, a 500 if the refund fails, and an audit row. `charge.refunded` for that refund finds the audit row or nothing; both are no-ops.
- Payout math: `calcSplit` matches the locked rules exactly (commission = round(15% of price), payout = price − commission + shipping, fee = ceil((2.9% × (price + shipping) + 30¢) / 0.971)); the three `DECISIONS.md` examples reproduce ($3.30, $30.18, $149.64); `transfer_data.amount` is the same locked payout the webhook records; the conservation invariant is tested.
- Refund math: partial refund of price + shipping + their tax, exact reversal of the recorded payout; the platform is left holding precisely fee + fee tax; idempotency keys and persisted Stripe ids make retries and concurrent settles converge.
- `charge.refunded` ignoring partial refunds is consistent: the platform's own refund is always partial and the admin route closes the order itself.
- Refund on an open dispute: Stripe rejects refunds on disputed charges, so `disputed → refunded` via the refund webhook cannot occur.
- Dispute-lost reversal: idempotency-keyed on the dispute id and gated on `stripe_reversal_id`, so a redelivery or a prior refund's reversal cannot double-reverse.
- Price, fee or commission edited mid-checkout: session metadata locks every amount; `buildOrderRecord` never reads the live listing and refuses to fabricate missing amounts.
- Listing deleted mid-checkout: deliberate loud 500 plus Sentry; the artist's transfer capability revoked mid-checkout: Stripe fails the payment before any order exists.
- Money and evidence columns: `00042` freezes every amount, both parties' ids, the Stripe ids, `refund_approved_at`, the snapshot columns, `signature_confirmed`, `protection_status`, `dispute_*`, both pickup stamps and `shipped_at` for non-privileged writers; buyers have no UPDATE policy on `orders`; the INSERT policy was dropped in `00038`.
- `approve-refund`: artist-only, CAS on `refund_approved_at IS NULL`, refuses non-open statuses.
- `confirm-pickup`: atomic per-party stamp, heals the both-confirmed-but-paid case, excludes `disputed` and refund-in-flight.
- Commission transitions: no client UPDATE policy; every route is ownership-checked and CAS-guarded on the source state; no money moves in commissions.
- `services/orders.ts` `updateOrderStatus` uses `.select().single()` rather than the convention's `.select('id').maybeSingle()`; `.single()` errors on zero rows, so the failure surfaces and the caller toasts. Equivalent, not a violation.
- Stripe fee pass-through, destination charges, Express accounts, 14-day payout delay, service fee retained on refund: all settled decisions and left alone.
