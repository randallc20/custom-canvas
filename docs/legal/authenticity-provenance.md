## What artists must state

Every listing must accurately describe:

- **What it is** — original, numbered edition, open edition, or reproduction/print
- **Medium and support** — e.g. oil on canvas, screenprint on paper
- **Dimensions**, and depth where relevant
- **Year** made
- **Condition** — any damage, repair, restoration, or material ageing
- **Edition details**, where applicable — edition size, this piece's number, whether
  signed, whether the plate or file has been destroyed or retired

## The words we hold artists to

- **Original** — a unique work made by the artist's hand. There is exactly one.
- **Limited edition** — a stated, closed number of copies, each numbered. The edition
  size must be stated and must not later grow.
- **Open edition / print / reproduction** — a copy of an existing work, produced without
  a fixed limit. **It must say so in the title or the first line of the description.**
- **Studio assistance** — if others worked on the piece under the artist's direction,
  say so.
- **Found or assembled materials** — if the work incorporates another maker's object or
  image, say so, and confirm you have the right to use it.

Describing a reproduction as an original is grounds for immediate removal and account
closure.

## AI-generated and AI-assisted work

Custom Canvas sells work made by people.

- **Wholly AI-generated work is not permitted.** If the piece is essentially the output
  of a generative system, it does not belong here.
- **AI-assisted work is permitted, with disclosure.** Where a generative tool was one
  step inside a piece the artist authored — reference, ideation, or an element they then
  painted, printed, cut or worked over — the description must say so, and say what the
  artist contributed.

**Why this line.** Two separate problems sit behind it. Under U.S. law copyright
requires **human authorship**, so purely generated material carries no copyright and an
artist cannot pass a buyer rights they do not hold. And independently of copyright,
**not telling a buyer that work is AI-generated may be actionable misrepresentation**.
Disclosure protects the artist as much as the buyer.

The line is drawn between *generated* and *assisted* rather than banning AI outright
because an outright ban would sweep in ordinary digital tools and be unenforceable in
practice, while permitting generated work with disclosure would put Custom Canvas in the
business of selling uncopyrightable output as local original art.

**This must be asked at listing time**, not left for artists to volunteer.

## What Custom Canvas does and does not do

**We do:** review each artist before their shop goes live, looking at their profile,
story and listings; investigate authenticity reports; and remove work and close accounts
where a claim is substantiated.

**We do not:** appraise, authenticate, or independently verify any work, its
attribution, its condition or its provenance. Our review is a check on the seller, not
a certification of the art. No listing on Custom Canvas is a guarantee of authenticity.

That distinction is deliberate and should be stated plainly to buyers rather than
softened — implying authentication we do not perform is precisely the representation
that creates liability.

## Reporting a problem

Write to **support@customcanvas.shop** with the listing URL and what you believe is
wrong. For a **copyright** claim, use the DMCA & Copyright Policy instead.

Where we substantiate a misdescription on a completed sale, **we will refund the buyer
whether or not the artist agrees.** An artist's discretion covers a buyer who changed
their mind; it does not cover a piece that was not what the listing said it was. This is
stated the same way in the Terms of Sale, the Returns & Refunds Policy and Artist
Agreement §8.

## Resale and royalties

Custom Canvas is a primary market. We do not operate a resale market and do not
administer resale royalties. Buying a piece transfers the object, not its copyright.
